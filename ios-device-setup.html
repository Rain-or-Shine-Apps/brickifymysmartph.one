Brickify Guide

iOS setup

No factory reset needed on iPhone — Brickify's protection comes from Apple's own Family Controls framework, set up entirely within the app.

← Back to How it works

Brickify on iOS uses the Family Controls and Screen Time native controls Apple already provides, then adds extra features on top — automatically switching between schedules, and warning you if actions you don't want are taken.

Before you start: your child's Apple Account

Brickify works best when the phone is signed in to your child's own Apple Account, in your Family Sharing group. Then only you can switch off Brickify's Screen Time access, which also means Brickify's lock on deleting apps can't be lifted without you. If your child is under 13, they can't leave the group either. On your own iPhone:

  1. Go to Settings → your name → Family → Add Member. Create an Apple Account for your child, or invite them if they already have one.
  2. Go to Settings → Screen Time and tap your child's name to turn Screen Time on for them. If your child is 13 or older, this matters: without it they can leave your family group, which switches Brickify off.

Then sign your child's phone in to their Apple Account, not yours. If your teen has their own account outside your family group, Brickify still works, with one extra risk.

Setup

1

Install and buy Brickify

Download Brickify from the App Store on the phone that's about to become your child's. Tap "Buy Brickify" and complete the one-time purchase.

2

Family account

Brickify checks the phone is signed in to your child's account and asks you to approve it. Enter your own Apple Account password, not your child's. This is the Family Controls permission that lets Brickify block apps. No child account? Tap "My child doesn't have their own account" and carry on.

3

PIN

Brickify's own 4-digit passcode — one you'll remember but won't enter on impulse.

4

2nd factor

Parent PIN, authenticator app, or email code — backs up your passcode so losing one doesn't lock you out for good.

5

Recovery codes

Three one-time backup codes. Write them down — they won't be shown again.

6

Profile

Name it and choose which apps it blocks. If you didn't approve Brickify on a child account in step 2, this is where iOS asks for Family Controls permission on the phone's own account.

7

Notify email

Where Brickify emails you if something needs attention — the device goes silent, its protection gets removed, or (despite blocking) a new app appears.

8

Screen Time

The last onboarding step walks you to Settings → Screen Time. Turn on "Use Screen Time Passcode" (a 4-digit code the child doesn't know — you can reuse the same digits as your Brickify passcode from step 2, one PIN to remember instead of two), then enable Content & Privacy Restrictions and set "Deleting Apps" to Don't Allow. On a child account you can also do this from your own iPhone, under Settings → Screen Time → your child's name.

9

Unlock Brickify

Pay, or Restore Purchases if you already bought it on this Apple ID.

Why the Screen Time passcode matters: deleting Brickify removes every block it set. While Brickify is running, iPhone won't let any app be deleted, but a Screen Time passcode with Deleting Apps set to Don't Allow is a second lock that doesn't depend on Brickify.

⚠️ The parent's Apple ID matters

Family Controls is tied to whichever Apple ID set it up. If your child knows your Apple ID password, they can remove the restriction directly via Settings → Screen Time on their own, bypassing Brickify entirely. Use a strong, unique password your child doesn't know.
