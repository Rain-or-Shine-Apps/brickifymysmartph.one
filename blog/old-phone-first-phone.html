The old phone in your drawer is your kid's first phone (you just don't know it yet)

Too good to throw away, too distracting to hand over. Every family has one.

2 October 2026 · By Anne Mundy

← Back to Parenting & phones

Ofcom's 2026 report found smartphone ownership jumps from 56% at age 10 to 83% at age 11. The move to secondary school is the moment most families give in. Most of us already have the hardware: an upgrade-cycle iPhone or Android sitting in a drawer. The problem was never the phone. It's what's on it, and when.

Brickify turns that phone into a brick phone: calls, texts and whatever you've both agreed. Then it moves to a more open profile at the times you've set. It's £4.99 once. It works on iPhone 8 / iPhone X or newer (iOS 16+) and Android 8.0 or newer.

You still need a SIM, a decent battery and I'd advise a factory reset before you hand it over. Also, if it no longer gets security updates, think twice. But despite this, Brickify is much easier than a brick phone and definitely safer than an open phone.

Critical thinking check

"Aren't you just giving an 11-year-old a smartphone by the back door?"

Yes, it's a smartphone, and that's ok. You decide what's switched on. I'd argue a smartphone with the internet off beats a brick phone: your child keeps the phone they already know how to use, and you can open it up gradually as they earn more trust.

Parents who want no smartphone until 14 should do that. Brickify is for those who've decided a phone is needed but want to limit it to the apps they think are safe.
