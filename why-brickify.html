Why I built Brickify

"My sons' school went smartphone-free, there was an old phone in a drawer, and they were ready for something. So I built Brickify."
Anne Mundy, founder, engineer (Cambridge MEng) and mum

I'd promised my boys a safe phone for a few apps at weekends. Nothing on the market did what I wanted, so I built it: a phone that's a brick on school days, opens up when the family decides, and changes as they grow.

Why families choose Brickify

Schools are phone-free by default. Brickify turns the phone already in the drawer into a brick phone on the family's schedule, and it can get smarter as they grow.

Since September, schools in England are expected to be mobile phone-free by default, and Ofsted now checks phone policies at every inspection. In the US, at least 39 states now require schools to restrict phones. Most parents welcome it. But school rules stop at the gate. The walk in, the bus home, the evening and bedtime are still ours to sort out, and a lot of families are reaching for a brick phone.

Before Brickify: Big Sister

My first idea was much more permissive. "Big Sister" left the phone open and flagged dangers as the kids used it.

Testing it changed my mind. There were just too many dangers, and following up after something bad had already happened wasn't good enough for my own children. So Brickify works the other way round: the phone starts as a brick, and opens up on a schedule the family chooses.

I've also written a public page listing exactly what Brickify can't stop, and how those risks are reduced. If a product for children can't tell you its limits, don't trust it.
